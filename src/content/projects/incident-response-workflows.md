## Overview

During an incident, responders juggle chat, tickets, and runbooks while the clock runs. I designed a playbook builder and a live incident room that unified them.

## The problem

Automation was powerful but required scripting. Most teams had one engineer who could build playbooks — a single point of failure.

## Users & context

Incident commanders, responders, and automation engineers working under extreme time pressure.

## Constraints

Playbooks had to be auditable, versioned, and safe to run in production.

## Discovery

We ran tabletop exercises with three customers and recorded every action. Patterns emerged: the same 12 steps made up 80% of all playbooks.

## Key decisions

### 1. Blocks, not code

A visual builder with typed inputs and outputs, with code available as an escape hatch.

### 2. Human-in-the-loop checkpoints

Destructive actions pause for approval, with the evidence attached.

## The solution

A canvas-based playbook builder and an incident room with a live timeline, task assignments, and one-click actions.

## Impact

- **4,200 playbooks** created by customers in the first year.
- **−45% time to containment** for teams using the incident room.

## Reflection

Designing for stress means designing for recovery — undo, audit trails and clear state matter more than speed alone.
